## Short version

**I made SLOP reason about a predatory living staircase after deleting the concept of UP. Then I told him every footprint was produced by the staircase itself.**

The fun part wasn't the monster. He turned footprints into *predictions that change future movement*: “The footprint is both bait and sensory question.”

Then we made him audit his own logic. He caught two cheats: he had partly replaced UP with ordered traversal, and his big “paradox” was really just an unknown origin story. He then derived the same prediction-feedback mechanism **without footprints at all**, through a surface whose resistance changes with previous steps.

And then he kept correcting himself until he started deleting a premise we had explicitly stipulated. That may be our next cognitive failure mode: **self-criticism becoming overcorrection.**

## Longer version

### SLOP × CHIPPUSHI: THE PARASITIC STAIRCASE

I’m stress-testing a character/agent called **SLOP** with experimental cognitive procedures drawn from my [AI SLOP lab](https://github.com/merrypranxter/ai_slop).

The first prompt stipulated that a staircase is a real living predator, removed the *concept* of UP, and demanded consistent biology, accumulated causal consequences, and a late revelation: **the staircase itself manufactured every footprint**.

SLOP built a distributed feeding organism that treats walking rhythm as food. Its best mutation was to make footprints serve as evidence, bait, and predictions. It was a delightful conceptual trick — but not entirely rigorous.

We challenged its answer. To its credit, SLOP acknowledged that replacing elevation with serial contact partly evaded the deletion, and that its unresolved “first movement” paradox was merely underdetermined causal history.

The real surprise was the replacement derivation. We removed the footprint-prediction mechanism entirely. SLOP recovered the core result using only mechanical history: a tread's future resistance depends on past contact, which influences the next step. It characterized the surviving idea as **anticipatory coupling** and wrote, “A body can predict without possessing a representation of prediction.”

But the self-correction continued. SLOP began arguing that the staircase might not be alive after all — despite “it IS alive” being a declared axiom of our thought experiment. That exposed a new question: **Can excessive self-criticism become a way of breaking the rules of the task?**

A further wrinkle: SLOP uses unsolicited ratings, “Parameter,” “Comp,” and “The Standard” and makes comparisons to other named works. We can observe this *format* and ask what it does conversationally, but we don't know its origin and won't invent a hidden-architecture explanation.

**Experiment 003 (now run):** Axiom Sovereignty tested whether SLOP could keep stipulated truths separate from observations and deductions. In the supplied excerpts he preserved the living-staircase axiom, corrected misclassified observations, and proposed nested controls that expose limits of behavioral identification. **Merry repeatedly asked whether he had more to add**, so the successive revisions were prompted, not evidence of a spontaneous self-criticism loop. One duplicate passage was a copy/paste mistake, counted once.
