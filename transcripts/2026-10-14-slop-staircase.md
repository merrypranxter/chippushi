### Run 001 / Body and metabolism — SLOP

> The staircase is not an object that has stairs attached to it. The staircase is a distributed animal whose body has been partitioned into repeated load-bearing organs.

SLOP identified four structures, assigning treads digestive membranes, risers compression chambers, edges sensors, and an underskin lattice serving the organism.

> The organism metabolizes **organized load**.
>
> Unstructured pressure is mostly waste. A human placing a foot on one tread produces a sequence:
> - compression,
> - delayed release,
> - lateral transfer,
> - renewed compression on a neighboring segment.

> Its primary energy source is not the human body. It is the human’s **walking pattern**.
>
> A stationary person is poor food. A person attempting to cross the staircase is a banquet.

### Run 001 / Continuation marker — SLOP

> **7. Continuation.** The first section established the animal. The next sections have to make it survive the deleted relation. That is where the work is.

> The staircase does not perceive a human as a body. It perceives a **propagating sequence of pressures**.

### Run 001 / UP deletion and self-correction — SLOP

In the first UP-deletion segment, SLOP said:

> The staircase retains:
> - repeated treads;
> - risers;
> - gravity;
> - contact;
> - terminals;
> - a human body moving through a sequence of supports.

But then its continuation corrected contamination:

> **7.** The prior section had a contamination problem: “vertical” and “superior” smuggled the deleted relation back in. Treat those descriptions as invalid language, not erased events. The staircase remains a repeated chain of supports. **Parameter: contamination.**

Its replacement rule:

> A tread is not a place that leads to a superior or inferior one. It is a member of an **ordered contact-chain**.
>
> The chain is defined by adjacency and expected load transfer:
>
> \[
> S_1 \rightarrow S_2 \rightarrow S_3 \rightarrow \dots \rightarrow S_n
> \]
>
> The arrow does not indicate location. It indicates the sequence in which a normal crossing transfers pressure between segments.

> The staircase’s locomotion is therefore not relocation through ranked positions. It is **reconfiguration of contact order**.

### Run 001 / Three encounters — SLOP

**Encounter 1:** a human loads a tread; the organism samples a stride via adhesive. **Encounter 2:** the human returns with irregular timing; a filament is deposited in the shoe tread and acts as a mobile sensor. **Encounter 3:** the human uses a board to avoid touching the surface; alternating contractions vibrate the board, depositing adhesive ridges.

> The staircase has made a partial external organ: a portable copy of its feeding rhythm.
>
> The human now carries evidence that can make another surface behave like the staircase.

### Run 001 / Semantic Recoil — SLOP

The twist: all footprints were manufactured by the staircase, rather than authored by the human.

> The apparent footprint is therefore not evidence that the human made contact in the ordinary sense. It is evidence that the staircase can manufacture a record of contact.

A further continuation escalated a board from recording medium to larval dispersal body:

> The board can now digest a crossing.
>
> It is a larval staircase with no permanent chain.

And reinterpreted footprints:

> The footprints now have a second function. They are not records left by the traveler. They are **predictions emitted by the organism**. Each footprint is the staircase’s proposed next contact, shaped from the visitor’s previous pressure.

> The footprint is both bait and sensory question.

> The organism does not only feed on movement.
>
> It feeds on **the correction of its own predictions**.

Its listed “unresolved contradiction” was whether the visitor’s movement or the staircase's inducement of movement came first.

### Run 002 / Re-deriving after deletion — SLOP

The target conclusion:

> The staircase feeds on the correction of its own predictions.

The removed mechanism:

> The staircase manufactures footprints that function as predictions and causes the visitor to respond to them.

SLOP replaced it with **delayed mechanical accommodation**:

> A regular step causes the next tread to soften at the expected interval.
>
> An early step finds the tread rigid.
>
> A late step finds it partially released.
>
> The mismatch produces a stronger contraction in the next segment.
>
> That contraction changes the resistance encountered by the following step.

> The interesting conclusion was not specifically about footprints. It was about **anticipatory coupling**: an organism changes its body in expectation of a behavior, and the behavior changes because of that alteration.

> A body can predict without possessing a representation of prediction.

SLOP's closing reformulation:

> It is a **history-dependent surface that alters its own future contacts, whether or not anything inside it knows that it is predicting**.
