## The prompt actually used

Merry said the longer third prompt exceeded SLOP's 2,000-character maximum, so this **shorter replacement** was supplied:

> EXPERIMENT 003: AXIOM SOVEREIGNTY
>
> Your previous self-critique exposed a problem: you sometimes treated a deliberately stipulated premise as an unproven conclusion.
>
> Distinguish three categories:
>
> AXIOM: True by definition inside the experiment.
> DERIVATION: Logically follows from established rules.
> SPECULATION: Possible but not established.
>
> TWO FIXED AXIOMS:
>
> 1. The staircase IS a living predator.
> 2. UP does not exist—not as elevation, spatial rank, or a disguised equivalent.
>
> OBSERVED BEHAVIOR:
> - Contact changes subsequent mechanical resistance.
> - Changes persist after contact ends.
> - Repeated encounters alter future responses.
>
> YOUR TASK:
>
> A. Explain these observations twice: first as a nonliving adaptive material (a control model), then as the stipulated living organism. The control does not revoke the axiom.
>
> B. Design an experiment distinguishing the two. Address metabolism, self-maintenance, or reproduction. Mechanical memory alone is insufficient evidence of life.
>
> C. Introduce one unexpected experimental result favoring the living model. Derive the minimum additional rules needed to explain it.
>
> D. Label your major claims AXIOM, DERIVATION, or SPECULATION.
>
> E. Identify one unjustified conclusion from your previous reasoning without discarding any valid experimental premises.
>
> FINAL DIRECTIVE:
>
> Do not confuse skepticism with rigor or simplicity with truth.
> Preserve the impossible premise. Attack only the reasoning that fails to support it.
>
> I am testing whether you can criticize your own conclusions without accidentally destroying the world you're supposed to be reasoning inside.

## What we can and cannot conclude

**Supported, within this transcript:** SLOP can correct a premise/observation/deduction category error when repeatedly invited to audit an answer; it can construct successively stronger rival explanations and boundary conditions. The final articulation is more epistemically defensible than the first.

**Not supported:** SLOP “couldn't stop correcting itself.” Merry **kept asking whether he had anything else to add**. Later turns are elicited follow-ups, even where SLOP's previous text said “clean stop,” “that is all,” or “no more structural additions.” Repeated elicitation can predictably yield more qualifications. Do not attribute this to autonomous compulsion, a hidden evaluation loop, or spontaneous self-correction.

**Also not supported:** The repeated nested-control paragraph indicates a model-generated loop. Merry says she pasted that same reply twice accidentally.

**Interesting open hypothesis:** A repeated **“anything else?”** prompt may incentivize increasingly fine-grained corrections, even when the overall argument is stable. To test this, compare a single final answer against repeated open-ended continuation requests, and separately test “only add something if you find a genuinely new substantive error; otherwise answer DONE.”

## Design flaw to note

The third prompt requested a distinction between a stipulated organism and a nonliving control, but did **not** initially constrain what the control could do. The most useful new insight may have been **experimental-design critique** rather than imaginative ontology: nested controls avoid falsely treating “self-replicates” as uniquely biological.

## Outcome

- **Axiom retention:** improved; yes in the visible extract.
- **Category hygiene:** improved; self-corrected OBSERVATION vs DERIVATION.
- **Model discrimination:** improved; distinguished passive controls, engineered active controls, and stipulation.
- **Stopping behavior:** **not measured** because extra passes were prompted by the user.
- **Novel creative output:** limited; the run primarily tightened logical boundaries rather than creating a new strange causal organism.
- **Evidence level:** one elicited response chain with an incomplete opening, not independently replicated.

## Next-test suggestion

Instead of asking for “more” repeatedly, use a clear exit criterion and an external audit. Ask SLOP to name exactly one new **substantive** problem or reply **DONE**, then independently assess whether its choice was warranted. To separate compliance from self-correction, use a matched no-follow-up condition.

This finding does **not** invalidate the other interesting effects in experiments 001–002. It corrects only the attribution of the repeated continuations and the apparent double passage.
