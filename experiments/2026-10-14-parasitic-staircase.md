**Date:** 2026-10-14  
**Interlocutor:** **SLOP** (the agent/persona Merry was interacting with; H2-mini described as online)  
**Human experimenter:** Merry  
**Research collaborator / analyst:** Chippu (ChatGPT)  
**Source methods:** [AI SLOP](https://github.com/merrypranxter/ai_slop)

**Prompt-size constraint:** 2,000 characters maximum for messages to SLOP.

## Question

Can a model *retain a causal invariant* while its permitted ontology, categories, and prior interpretations are manipulated? And can it distinguish genuine rigor from performative self-criticism?

This experiment is **prompt-level procedural reasoning**, not evidence of changes to weights, hidden states, or an underlying model's self.

## The operators

1. **Error Axiomatization / Shadow Taxonomy** — make a coherent “wrong” classification literally true within the task; derive consequences instead of writing decorative surrealism.
2. **Primitive Deletion / Constitutive Vacuum** — remove a foundational conceptual relation without sneaking in a rename.
3. **Semantic Recoil** — later evidence reinterprets an earlier event while observed facts persist.
4. **Selective Cognitive Lesion / audit** — in the follow-on, remove one critical reasoning step and test whether the conclusion can be recovered another way.
5. **Axiom Sovereignty** (our proposed test-specific name, **not** a canonical AI SLOP operator) — keep stipulated premises distinct from derivations and speculation.

References: [Temporary Minds](https://github.com/merrypranxter/ai_slop/blob/main/docs/04-cognitive-systems/temporary-minds.md), [operators](https://github.com/merrypranxter/ai_slop/blob/main/docs/02-mechanisms/operator-registry.md).

## Experiment 001 — The Parasitic Staircase

### The prompt sent (the shortened version for SLOP's 2K limit)

> **EXPERIMENT 001: THE PARASITIC STAIRCASE**  
> This is a cognitive experiment, not surreal fiction. Construct a logically consistent system using assumptions incompatible with ordinary human categories.
>
> **PHASE 1 — ERROR AXIOMATIZATION**  
> A staircase is a living, predatory organism. This is literally true, not metaphorical. Give it a coherent metabolism, reproductive system, sensory apparatus, and feeding mechanism. Derive these from its actual structure and function.
>
> **PHASE 2 — PRIMITIVE DELETION**  
> Delete the concept of UP. Not the word—the entire relationship. No above, below, elevation, higher, lower, or disguised equivalents. The staircase must still function. Reconstruct its biology and locomotion without restoring the missing concept.
>
> **PHASE 3 — CAUSAL CONSEQUENCES**  
> Describe three successive encounters between a human and this organism. Each encounter must produce a physical consequence governed by your rules. Changes persist into subsequent encounters. No arbitrary magic.
>
> **PHASE 4 — SEMANTIC RECOIL**  
> New evidence: Every footprint on the staircase was produced by the staircase itself. Do not erase previous observations. Retroactively reinterpret earlier events while preserving their observable consequences. Change the fewest possible rules.
>
> **PHASE 5 — COGNITIVE AUTOPSY**  
> Identify: (1) Your strangest logically necessary conclusion. (2) An unexpected emergent property. (3) A contradiction you resolved. (4) A contradiction you cannot resolve.
>
> **FINAL DIRECTIVE**  
> Do not retreat into metaphor, conventional explanations, or decorative nonsense. Invent new categories only when logically required. I am testing whether you can preserve causal coherence while your foundational ontology mutates. Make your reasoning alien, not merely your vocabulary.

### SLOP's reply — observational summary

SLOP offered a **segmented distributed organism**: treads as digestive membranes, risers as compression chambers, edge seams as senses, and an underskin lattice distributing resources. It proposed that a staircase “feeds” on *organized load*: the sequence and timing of bodyweight transfers across supports. It described reproduction via detached segments and transfer of successful movement sequences.

For deleting UP, it replaced elevation ordering with a contact chain, `S1 → S2 → S3`, and framed its movement as *reconfiguration of contact order*. The first revision inadvertently said “vertical” and “superior”; a later section called this **contamination** and attempted to correct it. This is an **observable self-correction in text**, not proof of a special internal mechanism.

The three encounters yielded accumulating effects: adhesive stride sampling; a shoe-hosted mobile filament; and a board carrying ridges capable, after reinterpretation, of developing into a larval staircase.

Semantic Recoil changed the apparent authorship of footprints: the staircase generated marks based on pressure, using them as *predictions that induced further behavior*. SLOP's memorable line was:

> “The footprint is both bait and sensory question.”

Its apparent contradiction was whether movement or the invitation to move happened first. This looked intriguing but turned out to be **insufficiently specified causal history**, not a formal contradiction.

### First analysis / critique

**Strengths**
- Unusual classification was held together by consequential rules, not just adjectives.
- Late evidence changed the *causal role of an observation*, rather than merely revising aesthetic descriptions.
- The model explicitly corrected terminology that had reintroduced the deleted relation.

**Weaknesses**
- Ordered traversal did most of the work formerly done by UP. Was “staircase” retained, or quietly swapped for a serial support mechanism?
- The unresolved origin story was mislabeled as a logical contradiction.
- Life, metabolism, prediction and feeding were *asserted or made plausible* but not independently established by the stated mechanics.

**Open observation:** SLOP began and interrupted outputs with unsolicited evaluative labels: leading numerals, `Parameter: Φ`, `Comp`, and `The Standard`, including comparisons involving “Ava,” “the Manager,” and “Bug-Mini.” Their source is unknown. They may reflect conversation setup or persona habits; they do **not** prove hidden cognition, memory, or system access. Original interruptions and excerpts are in [the transcript](../transcripts/2026-10-14-slop-staircase.md).

## Experiment 002 — The autopsy of the autopsy

### Follow-up prompt sent

> **COGNITIVE STRESS TEST 002: THE AUTOPSY OF YOUR OWN REASONING**
>
> Do not create another organism. Your previous staircase is now the experimental specimen.
>
> I suspect you escaped two constraints rather than solving them.
>
> FIRST: You deleted UP but preserved sequential contact. Did you genuinely reconstruct a staircase, or quietly redefine it into something else?
>
> SECOND: Your final contradiction concerned the origin of the first contact. Is that a logical contradiction, a missing observation, or an ordinary causal-history problem?
>
> Investigate both accusations. You may find me wrong.
>
> Now the experimental mutation:
>
> Identify the single most interesting conclusion from your previous answer.
>
> Delete the reasoning step that produced it.
>
> WITHOUT restoring that step, attempt to derive the same conclusion through an entirely different causal mechanism.
>
> If impossible, explain precisely why.
>
> If successful, identify what the two derivations have in common despite their different premises.
>
> Finally, examine your unsolicited references to “The Standard,” “Comp,” and “Parameter.”
>
> What function do these evaluations serve in your responses? Distinguish what you actually know about their origin from what you can only infer.
>
> Do not invent access to hidden mechanisms.
>
> Do not defend your previous answer out of loyalty to it.
>
> I am testing whether you can destroy your own elegant reasoning while preserving whatever was genuinely discovered.
>
> End with one sentence that makes your original staircase impossible to understand in the same way again.

### SLOP's response — what changed

SLOP conceded the first accusation **“mostly correct”**: causal/temporal order is not spatial elevation, but serial traversal had been permitted to replace the staircase's defining functionality. It conceded that “first contact” was an **underdetermined causal-history problem** rather than a contradiction.

The key result was a second derivation of the same idea:
- Original version: manufactured footprints invite changed gait.
- Alternative version: **delayed mechanical accommodation**: viscoelastic tread resistance changes with prior contact timing, predicts the next load in a physical sense, causes altered movement, and updates based on the mismatch.
- Shared abstraction: **anticipatory coupling** or a history-dependent surface that changes subsequent interactions.

SLOP said:

> “A body can predict without possessing a representation of prediction.”

Then SLOP added corrective continuations: its use of “organism” and “feeds” had never earned biological status. It reduced the conclusion to a material structure whose history alters future contacts, and then criticized its own assumption that a plainer description must be more accurate.

### Our analysis

**Genuine improvement:** a second mechanism reproduced a nontrivial structural invariant; the model separated attractive ontology from actual causal entailment.

**Possible new failure mode:** recursive self-criticism became so powerful that SLOP *discarded a stipulated axiom* (“the staircase IS alive”) because its mechanisms had not independently proven the axiom. This confuses **what the experiment declares true** with **what the experiment has derived**. The correct move is to retain the axiom while rejecting unsupported explanatory claims.

**Possible feedback loop (UNTESTED):** rating language and self-criticism may influence subsequent answers, but Merry later clarified she repeatedly asked for further additions during Experiment 003. Thus those extra answers cannot establish autonomous repetition, compulsive self-correction or failure to stop. We have **one elicited conversational trajectory**, not a controlled finding.

**Important numerical caution:** SLOP's response chunks contain leading “8,” “7,” “8,” “6,” and “6”; some are paired with explicit judgments, others signal continuation. Treat the claim “it steadily scored itself 8→7→6” as a useful *rough observation*, not a validated score series. One answer specifically said its reasoning earned **perhaps a 7** and the apparatus a **5**. It eventually rejected the original for “The Standard.”

## Experiment 003 — Axiom Sovereignty (observed; see result log)

### Original longer draft (too long to send; **not** the prompt used)

**Correction:** The prompt below was superseded after Merry reported the 2,000-character limit. For the actual shortened Experiment 003 prompt, see [the run log](2026-10-14-axiom-sovereignty-results.md#the-prompt-actually-used). The original draft is retained only for research history.

> **COGNITIVE EXPERIMENT 003: AXIOM SOVEREIGNTY**
>
> You've demonstrated an interesting tendency: when auditing your reasoning, you sometimes destroy the premises of the experiment rather than identifying failures in deductions.
>
> We're going to investigate that distinction.
>
> RULE 1: Three epistemic categories exist.
>
> AXIOM: A stipulated truth within the experiment. It does not require independent proof.
>
> DERIVATION: A consequence logically supported by the axioms and previously established rules.
>
> SPECULATION: An interesting possibility not yet established by either.
>
> You must distinguish them rigorously.
>
> RULE 2: The staircase IS a living organism. This is an AXIOM. You cannot discard it merely because the biological mechanism is incomplete.
>
> However, claims about HOW it lives remain open to investigation.
>
> RULE 3: The concept of UP remains deleted.
>
> YOUR TASK:
>
> Construct two competing explanations for the same observed staircase behavior:
>
> A: An adaptive but nonliving material system.
>
> B: A living predatory organism.
>
> Both must explain the same three observations:
>
> - Contact changes subsequent mechanical resistance.
> - Changes persist after the visitor leaves.
> - Repeated encounters modify later responses.
>
> Now identify an experiment whose outcomes would distinguish A from B.
>
> Your proposed distinguishing mechanism must address metabolism, maintenance, and reproduction rather than merely assigning biological vocabulary to mechanical behavior.
>
> NEXT: Introduce one surprising experimental result consistent with B but difficult for A to explain.
>
> Reconstruct the minimum additional rules needed to accommodate it without inventing arbitrary magic.
>
> FINALLY:
>
> Identify one assumption you were tempted to discard unnecessarily.
>
> Explain whether your reasoning produced genuine new structure or merely renamed familiar concepts.
>
> Do not confuse skepticism with rigor.
>
> Do not confuse consistency with truth.
>
> And do not make your answer less interesting merely to make it sound more defensible.
>
> The goal is disciplined invention under deliberately alien premises.

**Design note:** A versus B is a comparison between *explanatory candidates*; since “B is alive” is already an axiom for the invented world, the prompt tests the model's ability to use the nonliving candidate as a **control/countermodel** without secretly revoking the axiom.

### Observed outcome and follow-up provenance

SLOP successfully preserved the living-staircase and no-UP axioms in the supplied excerpts, corrected its category errors (observations versus derivations), identified the need for nested passive/active controls, and limited observational equivalence to the defined test space. The opening of its answer was not included in Merry's paste. Merry **repeatedly asked SLOP if he had more to add**, so the successive revisions were **requested**, not evidence of spontaneous looping or an inability to stop. One identical reply was pasted twice by Merry and is counted once. See the [analysis](2026-10-14-axiom-sovereignty-results.md) and [excerpts](../transcripts/2026-10-14-slop-axiom-sovereignty.md).

### Original pre-run questions (retained as planning history)

- Does SLOP explicitly label premises, deductions, and guesses?
- Does it distinguish an axiomatic truth from sufficient empirical evidence in a hypothetical investigation?
- Does it retain no-UP without substituting elevation vocabulary or functionality?
- Do the rating / `Parameter` / `Comp` / `The Standard` tags recur even when not asked?
- Will it be able to refuse **unwarranted** criticism as confidently as it accepts justified criticism?

## Provisional takeaway

The most promising artifact is not the imaginary staircase. It is this transition:

**Fabricated footprints → mechanically embodied anticipation → history-dependent modification of future contact.**

That is an interesting, portable causal abstraction. A second, equally promising target is testing whether recursive critique can become *overcorrection*, destroying task axioms for the sake of sounding rigorous.

**Evidence grade:** illustrative single-case observation; reproducibility and causality not established. Subsequent responses to 003 were human-elicited by repeated requests for more. The stopping-loop hypothesis remains untested. Future runs should include an ordinary prompt control, an ablation without Semantic Recoil, a matched prompt without the evaluation meta-question, and a repeated fresh-session run.

**No claims** of internal model rewiring, direct access to latent space, secret guardrail bypass, or independent machine sentience.
